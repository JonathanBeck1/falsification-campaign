## 10. Related Work

This campaign is best read not as a discovery but as a *retail-friction boundary survey* of effects the literature has already documented. The recurring pattern is that the published premium is *real in our data too* and the original authors were not wrong; what the campaign adds is the demonstration that, on free data, at retail friction, EOD-marked, in the surviving liquid subsample, the effect is either decayed into the placebo band, consumed by single-name spreads, or revealed as compensated risk rather than alpha. Below, each family is positioned as CONFIRM (we reproduce the effect), REFINE (we sharpen the tradeability boundary the literature leaves implicit), or CONTRADICT (we overturn a specific prior claim). Citations carrying residual uncertainty are marked [verify].

### 10.1 Variance and correlation risk premia (T19, T22, T23, T24, T25, T32)

The variance-risk-premium literature — Carr & Wu (2009), Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009), Bakshi & Kapadia (2003), Coval & Shumway (2001), and the CBOE PUT/BXM put-write series — establishes that implied variance systematically exceeds realized. We **confirm** this in both equity (QQQ) and crypto (BTC: +8.9 vol-points, IV>RV on 73% of days). Our **refinement** is to separate *existence* from *capturable alpha*, which this literature tends to conflate. On retail-accessible equity index options the after-cost daily-condor harvest (T19) is not merely small but mechanistically *null* — the IV−RV↔P&L correlation is +0.01 with a flat quartile gradient — which sharpens the standard "VRP is compensation for jump/tail risk" reading into "what a retail short-vol proxy actually collects is short-gamma beta, not the premium." The one place the mechanism genuinely passes is crypto BTC-VRP (T32: corr +0.181, monotone, tail-surviving), precisely where the literature is youngest; and there we document the premium *decaying out of sample* (t=5.02 → 1.88 → ~1.0) exactly as the anomaly-crowding view of McLean & Pontiff (2016) predicts for a maturing market — the **reverse** of the "less-arbitraged crypto preserves the premium" thesis our own pre-registration entertained, and the closest thing in the campaign to a contradiction of a prior expectation.

The single-name vol surface (T22–T25) extends the same boundary. We **confirm** the descriptive core of each published effect — implied correlation exceeds realized (Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov 2009), IV overstates the earnings move (Barth & So 2014 [verify]; Dubinsky, Johnson & So 2019 [verify]), the term structure carries a largely single-factor short-vol slope (Johnson 2017 [verify]; Aït-Sahalia, Karaman & Mancini 2020 [verify]), and the 25-delta risk reversal is persistently negative (Bakshi, Kapadia & Madan 2003; Xing, Zhang & Zhao 2010 [verify]). We **refine** the tradeability claim each leaves implicit: at honest retail single-name friction and EOD marking, none survives. T22 is the sharpest refinement of Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov — we reproduce their correlation risk premium (implied 0.38 vs realized 0.35, gross t=+1.98) yet crossing 26 single-name straddles at *realized quoted half-spreads* costs 365% of the gross edge, turning a real premium into an after-cost −14%/month loss and corroborating the practitioner consensus that dispersion is a capacity-limited *desk* strategy with internalized flow and variance-swap access [verify], not a retail one. Unlike DMV's risk-based reading, the loss in our sample is *not* crash compensation (corr with index moves +0.03); it is pure transaction cost. T23, T24 and T25 independently **confirm** the more sophisticated strand that reads these premia as compensated short-vol/jump beta rather than mispricing: the residual alpha after stripping short-vol/short-delta beta is null or wrong-signed (T24 residual t=−0.18 with placebo p=1.000; T25 residual t=−33.56; T23 corr(P&L, move)=−0.84). The net position is a retail-friction *boundary case* for the surface-premium literature — these premia are real and, as the literature increasingly argues, are risk compensation, and that compensation is exactly what makes them uncapturable for a price-taker paying single-name spreads.

### 10.2 Event and announcement drift (T07, T39, T41, T42, T43)

This family — post-earnings-announcement drift (Ball & Brown 1968; Bernard & Thomas 1989, 1990), the index-reconstitution "index effect" (Shleifer 1986; Harris & Gurel 1986; Beneish & Whaley 1996), buyback-announcement drift (Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen 1995; Peyer & Vermaelen 2009 [verify]), and spin-off drift (Cusatis, Miles & Woolridge 1993; Greenblatt 1997) — is the most heavily documented corner of the anomalies literature, and a substantial follow-up literature already documents its post-2000 decay in liquid large caps (Greenwood & Sammon, "The Disappearing Index Effect" [verify]; McConnell, Sibley & Xu on spin-offs [verify]). We **refine** rather than contradict this consensus, by replacing the literature's blurred "it decayed" summary with four *distinct, individually diagnosed* reasons:

- **Index reconstitution (T39)** is not merely weak but *placebo-absorbed*: a same-name random-window control earns the same +0.41% (t=2.85), identifying the residual as recent-winner name drift rather than forced index-fund flow. This **refines** Greenwood & Sammon's "disappearance" into a positive identification of the residual.
- **Spin-off drift (T42)** is a clean, well-powered, placebo-confirmed *null* (t=−0.03, N=104, survivorship-safe), **confirming** the ETF/index-fast-track absorption story.
- **Buyback drift (T41)** is the sharpest refinement: it *survives* a generous pre-registered flat-cost gate (+1.08%, t=+3.39) and dies only under honest liquidity-tiered friction (−0.97%, t=−3.04), localizing Ikenberry-Lakonishok-Vermaelen's effect as a small-cap *illiquidity* premium uncapturable at retail friction rather than a tradeable signaling edge.
- **Earnings premium (T43)** decays from +0.46% (t=2.76, 2015–2020) to −0.01% (t=−0.07, 2021–2025) and correlates +0.73 with event-window volatility — compensation for the scheduled jump, not alpha, on a well-powered N=2,156 sample.

Our **PEAD (T07)** result is a methodological cautionary tale, *not* an estimate of the Bernard–Thomas effect and not a contradiction of it: because we sorted on the day-0 return rather than a standardized earnings surprise (SUE), the apparent +3.74% drift was pure day-0 look-ahead contamination that sign-flipped to −3.70% once the window started strictly after the cohort-defining day. It demonstrates how the canonical event-study pitfall reproduces a "famous anomaly" from thin air — a finding about *evaluation*, leaving the published SUE-sorted effect untouched.

### 10.3 Informed-trading cloning (T44, T45)

The literature locates a genuine informed-trading signal at the *trade* date, not the disclosure date a follower can act on: insider purchases earn abnormal returns (Lakonishok & Lee 2001 [verify]; Jeng, Metrick & Zeckhauser 2003 [verify]), with opportunistic/cluster buys the strongest sub-signal (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012 [verify]); and managers' highest-conviction 13F positions outperform (Cohen, Frazzini & Malloy, "Best Ideas" [verify]) but are eroded by the ~45-day disclosure lag under Rule 13f-1. We **refine** this by making the literature's own caveat *quantitatively binding*: anchored strictly to the public disclosure date, the after-cost cloned CAR is the *wrong sign* in both legs (insider clusters −1.86%, t=−0.83; 13F −0.56%, t=−2.09), loses to a same-name placebo, and flips sign out of sample, with the mean 47-day 13F filing lag isolated as the binding constraint exactly as pre-registered. The documented premium is real where the literature places it (the trade date) and absent where retail must trade it (the filing date) — a stale-information / limits-to-arbitrage effect, not a lack of skill. We flag honestly that the insider leg lost 84 of 207 clusters to a microcap survivorship purge, so this **refines** the effect in the *surviving liquid subsample* and does not falsify it in the neglected small-cap tail where Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski locate it.

### 10.4 Closed-end-fund discount and activist effects (T13, T15, T16)

The CEF discount is among the oldest documented anomalies: a sentiment/noise-trader phenomenon (Zweig 1973 [verify]; Lee, Shleifer & Thaler 1991 [verify]) with a mean-reverting, predictable component (Pontiff 1995, 1996 [verify]), persisting because arbitrage is *costly* (Pontiff 1996's "costly arbitrage" — idiosyncratic risk, holding costs, dividend/borrow frictions). On the activist leg, Bradley, Brav, Goldstein & Jiang (2010 [verify]) show campaigns to open-end or liquidate CEFs narrow the discount and earn abnormal returns concentrated in campaigns that actually force resolution. We **confirm** the half-century-old mechanism: Saba's 13D filings produce a real, post-disclosure +1.44% one-month pop (t=2.78, 65% positive) with a *negative* pre-run-up, exactly where the activist literature predicts. We **refine** the *trading* claim by operationalizing Pontiff's costly-arbitrage thesis at retail scale: the premium does not survive realistic CEF execution, netting to a +0.44% coin flip (48% positive) at a ~1% round-trip and turning negative at 1.5%, while the longer-horizon payoff is a convex lottery (median ≈ 0) carried by the minority of campaigns that resolve at NAV. The inefficiency persists *because* it is the arbitrage cost — wide spreads and slow, capacity-limited resolution make it uncapturable for a retail follower. We flag two honesty caveats the literature comparison must carry: the ~1% round-trip is *asserted, not measured* on the followed funds, so the T13 verdict is cost-sensitive in the 0.5–1.5% band; and the T15 broad-tilt "undeployable" verdict is a *prediction* from a 20-fund pilot that explicitly could not test capturability, not a completed matched-control study. T16 separately illustrates how a naive backtest ignoring these frictions inflates to a spurious +10.5% (t=2.45) built from asset-class beta against a mismatched benchmark plus a single +224% vendor glitch.

### 10.5 Crypto derivatives (T29, T31, T32, T33, T34)

The crypto literature documents each effect as genuinely present: perp funding carry (the BitMEX perpetual-funding tradition; basis/funding-carry framing [verify]), time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012; Liu & Tsyvinski 2021 [verify]), persistent-but-bounded cross-exchange dispersion (Makarov & Schoar 2020 [verify]), stablecoin-peg arbitrage (Terra/UST and USDC-SVB post-mortems; Lyons & Viswanath-Natraj [verify]), and a crypto VRP (Deribit/DVOL short-vol studies [verify]). We **confirm** every effect in the raw data — funding yields ~31.5%/yr gross, trend rules ~80%/yr, cross-venue dispersion at t=51, depeg re-pegs in survivors, BTC IV>RV. Our **refinement** is to carry each through to net, exposure-matched, survivorship-honest deployment, where each resolves into a *named* failure mode:

- **Funding carry (T29)** is ~78% repaid by the short-perp price leg — a compensated directional-risk premium netting to zero, **confirming** the skeptical "carry is warehousing risk, not free money" reading and resolved honestly on Binance's delisting-retaining archive (23% dead symbols; survivor-only ≈ cohort).
- **Momentum (T33)** is levered bull-market beta whose Sharpe (0.79) trails buy-and-hold BTC (0.99), with a random-timing placebo capturing ~91% of the return — **refining** Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen-style TSMOM as compensated exposure in this market.
- **Cross-exchange dispersion (T31)** is real but an order of magnitude below two-venue taker fees — the exact limits-to-arbitrage friction Makarov & Schoar emphasize, here pushed to its extreme (gross t=51, net t=−33).
- **Depeg (T34)** is a survivorship/tail artifact whose entire sign is set by the one stablecoin (UST) that died (+3.1% survivor-only → −16.0% with UST, N=4).
- **BTC-VRP (T32)**, the one mechanism-real survivor, is *decaying* out of significance as the market matures — again the **reverse** of the "young, less-arbitraged market preserves the premium" prior.

In short, the crypto literature's premia exist in-sample exactly as claimed; under honest friction, complete universes, and exposure-matched benchmarks they are compensated risk, beta-in-disguise, friction-scale noise, or a decaying premium — not accessible alpha.

### 10.6 Calendar and seasonality anomalies (T47–T50)

The calendar literature is today largely a literature about decay: turn-of-month (Ariel 1987; Lakonishok & Smidt 1988; McConnell & Xu 2008 [verify]), weekend/Monday and pre-holiday effects (French 1980; Gibbons & Hess 1981 [verify]; Ariel 1990), the Halloween/sell-in-May indicator (Bouman & Jacobsen 2002; Jacobsen & Zhang 2018 [verify]), and the pre-FOMC drift (Lucca & Moench 2015). The meta-literature is already skeptical — Sullivan, Timmermann & White (2001) show calendar effects do not survive a data-snooping bootstrap, and McLean & Pontiff (2016) find ~58% post-publication decay. We **confirm** the history and **refine** rather than contradict it: replicated on free daily SPY data through 2025 under one pre-registered gate, all four families are outright kills, exactly as the data-snooping and post-publication-decay views predict. Three (turn-of-month, pre-FOMC, day-of-week) die the same way — a respectable in-sample tilt (pre-FOMC IS t=2.08; TOM IS +5.8 bps) collapses out of sample and falls inside a random-window placebo band, with the Monday effect not merely vanishing but **sign-reversing** (Thursday is now the weakest weekday). Sell-in-May refines the literature in a sharper sense: the winter>summer split is directionally intact but in-noise (t=1.12), and decisively, the seasonal switching strategy's Sharpe (0.676) is *below* buy-and-hold (0.771) — its celebrated lower drawdown is reduced market exposure (50% in cash), not alpha. These effects were real once and are now, on retail-accessible instruments, either decayed into the placebo band or revealed as compensated exposure.
