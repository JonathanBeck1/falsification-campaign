## 9. Limitations and Threats to Validity

The headline of this paper is a *null*: across roughly forty pre-registered strategy families, no edge survived our gate *and* cleared deployment. A null is a fragile thing to publish, because the single most damaging objection to any negative result is not "your test was wrong" but "your test was *blind*" — under-powered to see an edge that was really there. We therefore lead with the caveat that governs all the others, and organize the remainder by the six lenses against which a hostile referee will press.

### 9.0 The caveat that frames everything: what "no edge" does and does not mean

The defensible claim of this work is narrow and we state it in one sentence: **no edge survived at retail friction, on EOD-marked free data, across the daily-frequency, liquid, single-underlying-heavy slice of strategy-space we were able to test.** It is *not* "the retail-accessible space is efficient," and it is emphatically *not* "markets are efficient." Everywhere the body or the slogans ("the boundary of accessible alpha," "a credible map of where the edges are not") reach past that sentence, they are rhetoric outrunning evidence, and §9.4 concedes the seams explicitly.

The dominant statistical risk for a null is **Type-II error** — declaring "no edge" when a small real edge exists but our apparatus could not resolve it. Because our gate is an explicit conjunction of seven independent conditions (§3), it is *deliberately* tuned to a high-Type-II / low-Type-I operating point: it is far more willing to discard a real-but-marginal effect than to bless a mirage. We argue below that this is the correct design for a *falsification* campaign with an asymmetric loss function — but it is also the campaign's largest honest weakness, and we own it rather than hide it.

### 9.1 Multiple testing and statistical power

This is the lens on which the paper is structurally strongest and rhetorically weakest, because until now it never named the issue at all.

**Multiple comparisons cut in our favor, and here is the argument made explicit.** Multiplicity inflates *false discoveries* — it manufactures spurious *survivors*, never spurious *nulls*. Under a global "no edge everywhere" hypothesis, ~40 independent tests at a per-test |t|≥2 bar would be expected to throw roughly two chance survivors. The campaign observed three marginals and killed or declined all three on grounds *orthogonal to the gate t-statistic* — friction (T13), decay and proxy risk (T32), and untested capturability (T15). A bias that can only invent survivors therefore cannot have produced a zero-survivor conclusion; if anything it should have produced *more* apparent survivors than we saw. Multiplicity can only have made the null look *better* than it is, which is the conservative direction for a negative claim. We concede the one place it genuinely bites: the surviving t-statistics are reported against an *uncorrected* per-test bar, and T13 (CEF, t=2.78) would not clear a family-wise (Bonferroni-style, ≈3.2 for 40 tests) threshold. Nothing turns on this — T13 is demoted on friction, not significance — but the bar is uncorrected and we say so.

**Power is the real exposure, and the well-powered mass of the campaign rescues most of it.** A demanding after-cost |t|≥2 bar means "no edge" can be indistinguishable from "too under-powered to see a small edge." For the high-N event studies this objection fails on the campaign's own evidence: the earnings-announcement premium (T43) is N=2,156, and its decision record states plainly that "the sample is well-powered; the kill is on the economics, not the data." At N>1,000 a |t|≥2 bar resolves effects down to a few basis points — well below any deployable threshold — so for that mass of the campaign "no edge" provably means "no edge *large enough to matter*," not "no power." The kills that rest on a *decisively negative after-cost sign* (T22 nets to t=−5.03; T31 to t=−33; funding carry flips +17→−17 bps/day) are likewise immune: underpower can fail to find a positive edge, but it cannot manufacture a robust, sign-stable negative.

**We concede the genuinely thin tests.** T34 (stablecoin depeg, N=4) is, as its own decision doc concedes, "an anecdote," and a kill there "does not prove no re-peg trade exists." The single-name option studies at N=53 monthly rebalances (T22, T24) and the in-noise seasonality decays (T47 t=1.35, T48 t=1.05, T49 t=1.12, T50 t=0.93) sit exactly in the band where a small real effect and pure noise are statistically indistinguishable. For these, we do not claim a confident zero. We claim something weaker and more honest: the kill survives *even granting low power*, because the after-cost sign is decisively negative (T22, T24 are negative even gross, with placebo p=1.000 for T24) or the decay is from a once-significant in-sample tilt into the placebo band (the seasonality cluster) — both patterns underpower cannot fake.

**Type-II dominance is the intended design point, not a flaw.** Because the gate is a seven-way AND, the probability that a *real* edge clears every independent step shrinks multiplicatively, making "miss a real edge" the structurally dominant error mode of the whole apparatus. We embrace this. The loss function of a falsification campaign is asymmetric: a false negative (skip a small real edge → do not trade → $0 lost) costs far less than a false positive (deploy a mirage → real capital lost). A high-Type-II / low-Type-I operating point is therefore *correct*, and the AND-stack is the deliberate mechanism that achieves it. The conjunction is also less lossy than it looks, because for a *genuine* edge the steps are not independent — a real effect tends to pass OOS, baseline, and permutation *jointly*, while an artifact fails one specific step. Every kill in the campaign names *one* disqualifying line, not death-by-a-thousand-thresholds, which is the empirical signature of a gate that discriminates rather than over-kills. The clinching rebuttal to "engineered to find nothing" is that the gate is demonstrably *not* pathologically over-killing: it passed BTC-VRP (T32) cleanly to a mechanism-positive t=5.10 (corr +0.18, monotone), and let the CEF pop (T13) register at t=2.78 with a confirmed negative pre-run-up. A gate that finds and reports its near-survivors is sensitive, not rigged.

### 9.2 Data quality and survivorship — including where an artifact could *mask* a real edge

The campaign treats survivorship and provenance as first-class threats (the ms→µs epoch bug, the 224% Tiingo glitch, the parity-derived "Sharpe 11," and the −319% split/spot fabrication were all caught by *verification*, not statistics). The harder question for a *null* is the inversion: could a data artifact be *masking* a real edge — manufacturing a false null rather than a false positive?

**The q=0 parity-spot assumption is load-bearing across the whole single-name vol-surface cluster, not just T26.** The parity inversion S ≈ K·e^{−rT}+(C−P) sets dividend yield q=0, so for the eight material dividend payers in the 24-name universe (PEP, TXN, CSCO, HON, QCOM, AVGO, LIN, TMUS) the derived spot is biased, and the ATM-strike selection, IV read, and straddle P&L in T22/T23/T24/T25 all inherit it. The paper surfaces q=0 only at T26, where the dividend *is* the finding. The honest defense — which we now surface from the source — is quantitative and strong: the parity-spot shift is ≈(q·T)·S, which is ≲0.2% for the universe's largest payers over the ≤120-day tenors used, *smaller than a strike increment*, so the ATM pick is unchanged and the kills do not turn on it. We concede the load-bearing breadth, audit it, and note that a one-line robustness re-run of T23/T24 on a forward-implied (q≠0) spot is the cheapest way to convert this from an unstated assumption into a closed question. Because the bias is largest precisely for dividend payers — over-represented in the loss tail of any short-straddle book — an unexamined q=0 could in principle be *deepening* the negative result and masking a thinner-but-real crush premium; the sub-strike-increment magnitude is what rules that out.

**The "survivorship biases survivors up, so the kill is conservative" argument is directional, not a bound.** §8.4 already concedes the event studies "repeatedly lost 40–80% of their universe to yfinance's delisting purge," and flags the neglected small-cap tail as the untestable frontier requiring a CRSP-grade panel. That posture is correct and we keep it. The gap is rigor: a 40–80% purge is not a minor conservatism adjustment, and the magnitude of its effect on the cohort mean is *unquantified on free data*. The direction claim ("purge biases survivors up") itself assumes the purged names would have drifted up in the cohort window — plausible for a long-into-distress strategy but unproven. We therefore reframe these kills precisely: **they are valid for the surviving liquid subsample and explicitly do *not* falsify the effect in the purged small-cap tail** where the insider-cluster, index-effect, and squeeze literatures historically located it. T44 is the cleanest illustration — 84 of 207 clusters were lost to the purge — and there the kill survives anyway, because the after-cost sign is *wrong even gross* (−1.46%) and sign-flips OOS, which the purge cannot have caused.

**The EOD-mark-as-intraday-execution proxy can mask a deployable conditioned variant.** The once-daily cadence (brain/30's documented caveat) means an intraday stop that breaches and reverts before the decision mark is never realized in the reported P&L. The bias direction is *not* innocent for the short-vol kills: an intraday stop the EOD mark cannot see would *improve* several short-vol books (T19, T24), so the EOD proxy could be masking a deployable conditioned version. We concede the verdicts are scoped to the EOD-marked, unconditioned implementation and do not extend to intraday-conditioned variants.

### 9.3 Friction and execution realism

The worry this lens tests is the inverse of the usual one: are costs assumed *too high*, manufacturing false nulls, and could a maker-rebate desk flip a "friction-scale" kill to alive?

**On the hard kills, the cost magnitude is not load-bearing, and our fairest cost model is the realized one.** The single-name option tests (T22/T23/T24/T25) charge *realized quoted half-spreads*, not a flat guess — the fairest possible model, immune to "you guessed too high." The binding gate line for most friction-scale kills is not cost at all: T31 is 35× over its 20-bps wall and window-independent; T22 is sign-stable in and out of sample at 3.65× the gross edge; T24/T44/T45/T39/T42/T43 die on sign-flips, wrong-sign-gross, mechanism-null, placebo-absorption, or |t|<2 — none of which a maker rebate touches. The most telling defense is T41, where the kill comes from *raising* costs (a tiered model up to 300 bps for sub-$0.5M/day names that the generous flat 15 bps waved away) — the exact inverse of the "costs too high" objection.

**We concede the non-uniform, untabulated equity-event cost model.** Across the EDGAR cluster the round-trip cost varies — T39 10 bps, T45 10 bps, T43 10 bps, T41 15 bps, T42 20 bps, T44 40 bps — with no table or stated rationale in the paper, which invites an "ad hoc / results-driven cost knob" charge even where no individual choice is wrong. The justification exists and we now state it: 10 bps for liquid mega/large-cap one-name CARs, 40 bps for the small-/neglected-cap insider universe, realized quoted half-spreads for the single-name option tests, 20 bps two-venue for crypto. Crucially, the *higher* 40-bps assumption on T44 is not load-bearing: T44 is killed on a wrong-sign gross (−1.46%, t=−0.83) and an OOS sign-flip, so the cost choice could not have been tuned to kill — the kill survives its own cost assumption being moved.

**The one cost number that is both verdict-flipping and merely asserted is the CEF round-trip (T13).** Unlike T22's realized spreads, the "~1% CEF round-trip" that demotes T13 from a clean t=2.78 signal to a +0.44% coin flip was *asserted* from "CEFs trade with wide spreads," never *measured* on the 62 Saba-followed funds. The decision doc itself lists "measure the +1m pop net of realistic CEF bid/ask" as unfinished work. A better-execution desk using limit orders inside the wide CEF spread — or capturing part of it as a liquidity provider — could plausibly face 40–60 bps, leaving +0.84–1.04% net and a genuine survivor. This is the single friction number where "too high manufactures a false null" could bite a verdict, and we concede the softness: the T13 verdict is cost-sensitive in the 0.5–1.5% band, and a measured-spread test is the one piece of unfinished due diligence on the campaign's strongest signal. We lead with the cost-*independent* part of the T13 kill — the 6m/12m median ≈ 0, convex-lottery shape carried by the ~24% of campaigns that resolve at NAV — so the verdict does not rest solely on the spread assumption.

### 9.4 Generalizability and scope

**The supported claim and the slogan are not the same, and only the supported claim is defended.** The title and the recurring phrase "the boundary of accessible alpha" assert over the entire retail-accessible space; the evidence covers the daily-frequency, liquid, single-underlying-heavy slice testable on free data. We resolve this in favor of the narrow claim everywhere: the campaign is *consistent with* broad accessible-space efficiency but does not *establish* it, and the boundary we map is the boundary "in the tested regime."

**The intraday/execution regime is claimed by infrastructure but never actually tested.** Despite owning ~1B rows of 1-minute QQQ options data, the campaign closed *zero* intraday-execution tests: T06, the only intraday lab, died on fabricated (parity-derived) data — a provenance artifact, not a strategy outcome — and the entire 0DTE cluster (T20 in-flight, T27/T28 planned) is unfinished. The qualifier "mostly daily-frequency" thus conceals that the intraday regime is a *hole*, not a sample. This matters because our own EMH prior predicts daily liquid large-cap is *most* arbitraged, which means the untested intraday / 0DTE / opening-auction / small-cap-momentum corners are exactly where the prior is weakest and a survivor is most plausible. The open clusters are not cosmetic; the verdict does not extend to sub-daily holding periods.

**The sample is convenience-driven by construction, and one whole side of every spread is untested.** The "collect-then-cancel" selection rule (§5) pulls what is free or sits on the current data tier, which systematically excludes families needing capital, fast data, or execution infrastructure: options market-making / liquidity provision (absent entirely), cross-asset and FX carry (absent), commodity/futures carry and term structure (T35–T38, planned), intraday stat-arb, and ML/alt-data (a single low-prior planned T51). The 40 tests are also concentrated — index options are nearly all on one underlying (QQQ), and the single-name vol cluster is N=53 monthly rebalances on a 26-name equal-weight QQQ *proxy*. We make no claim about the excluded families and flag them as planned precisely so the map is honestly incomplete rather than selectively stopped. The sharpest concession: the campaign repeatedly dies by *paying* the spread (T22, T31, T04), yet the symmetric question — can a retail-adjacent participant *capture* it as a liquidity provider? — is never asked. We tested one side of every spread.

### 9.5 The marginals — are we under-claiming a real edge?

A fair referee steelmanning the bull case has genuine ammunition, concentrated on one family.

**BTC-VRP (T32) passed the full signed gate; its demotion is a deployment judgment layered on top of a gate pass, not a gate failure.** This is the campaign's sharpest under-claim exposure, and we confront it directly. The signed pre-registration fixes a closed gate and a closed kill list. BTC clears every gate line — after-cost tail-inclusive t=+5.10, OOS same *sign* (IS +0.0879, OOS +0.0300), mechanism corr +0.181 and monotone, placebo null, drop-worst-3 still t=6.56 — and trips *none* of the §8 kill criteria. The three reasons for non-deployment (decay trajectory, single-asset fragility, variance-swap-proxy-vs-margined-book) are *supplementary* considerations not in the pre-registered set. "OOS t<2 on a sub-sample" is a *new, harsher* bar than the signed "OOS same-sign," which BTC passes. We therefore concede the precise honest framing: **BTC-VRP passed the falsification gate; we declined to deploy it on documented forward-risk grounds.** That is a deliberate two-stage structure — gate-pass, then a separate deployment decline — not a goalpost move, and we state it as a gate pass rather than burying it under "marginal." The decay read deserves the same precision: the *primary* pre-registered metric is the full-sample tail-inclusive t=+5.10, which passes overwhelmingly; the "decaying" demotion relies on a 2025-only sub-window the gate never specified. As the decision doc itself says, the kill "does NOT prove crypto VRP never existed (it clearly did, richly, in 2021–2023)." The honest statement is forward-looking: BTC-VRP *was* a deployable edge in 2021–2023 and is compressing; we decline to deploy *forward* because the trajectory points down and we cannot test the next COVID-class spike (DVOL begins 2021-03-24). "It decayed" is not "it was never there," and the paper must stop letting "decaying" do the work of "killed."

**T13's kill rests on an unmeasured cost while we measured costs rigorously elsewhere.** As in §9.3: the campaign charges realized spreads for T22 and a tiered liquidity curve for T41, but demotes its *best* signal on an assumed 1% round-trip with explicit sensitivity (positive below 1%, negative above 1.5%) and no measurement of the followed-fund spreads. This is a genuine methodological inconsistency, and "real but undeployable" is premature for T13 until the realized spreads are pulled.

**T15's "undeployable" is a prediction, not a tested result.** T15's verdict traces to a 20-fund free pilot (10 live + 10 delisted) whose go/no-go doc states plainly that capturability "is untouched here," there is "no control group," selection was "survivorship-conditioned... hand-picked," and "the single most probable outcome is the matched-control study showing real-but-uncapturable a third time." So T15 is a base-rate extrapolation from T13, not a completed matched-control test. Listing it as a settled "marginal" in a results table overstates the evidence; the definitive T21 resolution-selection study is in-flight *because* the question is open. The discount-narrowing *mechanism* is confirmed (7 of 8 dead funds narrowed, aggregate −5.0%→−2.9%), but the broad tilt has not been *falsified* at deployment — only predicted to fail.

**The headline must distinguish "passed the gate" from "cleared deployment."** Restated honestly, the result is: of ~40 families, the falsification gate passed exactly one (BTC-VRP) on every pre-registered line including tail survival, and the campaign declined to deploy it on forward-risk grounds. That is materially less sweeping than "no edge survived rigorous testing." We enforce one consistent distinction everywhere — *no edge survived* **and** *cleared deployment* — which turns a "you under-sold your own survivor" attack into a strength: the gate is sensitive enough to pass a real premium, and the campaign is disciplined enough not to over-deploy a decaying single-name proxy.

### 9.6 Taxonomy and narrative coherence

**The taxonomy is not the closed, pre-specified scheme the prose implies, and the honest version of that fact is itself a finding.** The original brain/30 taxonomy enumerates exactly *seven construction-artifact* classes (look-ahead, friction-scale, data-glitch, derived-data, pooled-baseline, composition, classifier-hindsight). Two of the paper's load-bearing buckets are not among them: §4.7 "compensated-risk / beta-in-disguise" (6 kills) and the §7.2 "decay / in-noise" bucket (8 kills — the *largest*). The decision records say so verbatim — the T33 doc: "Not one of the seven artifact classes per se"; the T32 doc: "Not an artifact in the brain/30 taxonomy sense." We therefore drop the claim that every kill "without exception belongs to one of the classes below," which is true only because the list was expanded until nothing fell outside it — the signature of a catch-all. The honest reframe: the campaign began with seven construction-artifact classes and *empirically discovered two further classes* that are not construction errors at all but **correct measurements of the wrong quantity** (decay-into-noise; compensated-risk/beta). That 7→9 expansion is the most important methodological result of the campaign, not a bookkeeping convenience.

**"Friction-scale noise" has quietly become a second catch-all.** Its seven members span heterogeneous mechanisms: T04/T31 are pure friction-scale (positive gross, cost eats it), but T23 is compensated jump-variance, T25 self-labels "friction-scale noise + disguised-beta," T41 is a small-cap illiquidity mirage that *sign-flips OOS* (a decay signature), and **T24 cannot be friction-scale by the §4.2 definition** — friction-scale requires a positive gross edge that cost consumes, but T24's own decision record states it "loses at zero friction," its slope signal is null (placebo p=1.000, "random ranks beat the slope"), and it "adds zero alpha over an unconditional short-vol book" (residual t=−0.18). T24 is a compensated-risk / signal-nullity kill, not a cost kill; the current label is contradicted by its cited source. We reclassify T24 (and footnote T23/T25 with the secondary classes their docs name) and report the §7.2 structural observations with a sensitivity note: even if T23/T24 move to compensated-risk, the qualitative claim is unchanged — decay (8) and compensated-risk (6→8) dominate; construction artifacts remain the minority (friction-scale 7→4–5). Showing the conclusion is robust to the bucketing judgment defuses the "the spine is an artifact of generous bucketing" attack.
